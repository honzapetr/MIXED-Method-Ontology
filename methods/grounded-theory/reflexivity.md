# Grounded theory – reflexivity

> What reflexivity means in grounded theory, for the researcher and for the model. Written for GT only; overlaps
> with `methods/reflexivity.md` and other methods are kept on purpose (`methods/README.md`). Status: draft (T10),
> from summaries; to check against Glaser & Strauss, Strauss & Corbin and Charmaz (`sources.md`).

## Stance by version

- **Classic (Glaser):** the danger is *forcing* – imposing preconceived concepts on the data. The researcher
  delays the literature, writes preconceptions down and lets categories emerge; *theoretical sensitivity* (the
  ability to see what is relevant) comes from immersion and from memos, not from a framework.
- **Straussian (Strauss & Corbin):** sensitivity is developed on purpose (questioning, comparisons, the flip-flop
  technique); the researcher's experience and the literature can sensitise if they are checked against the data.
- **Constructivist (Charmaz):** data and theory are co-constructed by the researcher and the participants;
  reflexivity is part of the method. The researcher's position, privileges and assumptions are examined and
  written into the analysis.

The study states which version it follows; the version decides what is done with the researcher's preconceptions
(set aside, used carefully, or made part of the account).

## The model in a GT study

- A model brings a large stock of ready-made concepts. In GT this is the risk of forcing at scale: the model tends
  to name codes with familiar sociological terms before the data earn them. Open codes by a model stay close to
  the words of the data (gerunds, in-vivo codes) and a concept from the literature is flagged as such.
- Constant comparison is the researcher's work; the model may propose comparisons but does not decide that two
  incidents belong to one category.
- A model has no theoretical sensitivity of its own in the GT sense (it was not immersed in the field); what looks
  like sensitivity is its training data. Its positionality memo says which fields it likely knows well.

## By phase

| Phase | Researcher | Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| before open coding | memo of preconceptions (classic: to set aside; constructivist: to examine) | declares its prior knowledge of the field and the material |
| open-coding | are my codes the participants' or mine? | marks codes taken from literature vs. from the data; notes merges it did not make |
| axial-coding | which relations do I expect because of my position? | notes relations it proposed from familiar models rather than from incidents |
| selective-coding | is the core category the data's centre or my interest? | revisits its positionality memo; compares with another model where available |
