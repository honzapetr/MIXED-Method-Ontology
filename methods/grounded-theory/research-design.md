# Grounded theory – research design

> How grounded theory links the purpose of a study, its research questions and its theory. Written for GT only;
> overlaps with other methods are kept on purpose (`methods/README.md`). The versions of GT differ; a topic says
> which one it follows. Status: draft from secondary summaries – to be checked against the works in `sources.md`.

## What GT is for

Grounded theory builds a theory of a social process or phenomenon from the data by coding, constant comparison,
memoing and theoretical sampling, until the categories are saturated. It is used where existing theory does not
explain the area well or where the researcher wants to understand how people act and make sense in it.

## Versions

- **Classic (Glaser & Strauss 1967; Glaser):** theory emerges from the data; the researcher enters the field
  without a preconceived problem and keeps the literature away until late.
- **Straussian (Strauss & Corbin 1990):** a structured procedure – open, axial (paradigm model: conditions,
  context, strategies, consequences) and selective coding.
- **Constructivist (Charmaz 2006/2014):** the theory is constructed by the researcher in interaction with the
  data and the participants, not discovered; reflexivity is part of the method.

## Purpose and goals

To understand a process in an area of interest: what is going on, how people handle it, with what consequences.
The goal is a theory useful for the people of the area and for the discipline.

## How research questions arise

- Classic GT: no preconceived question – an area of interest and "what is going on here?". The main concern of
  the participants emerges from the data.
- Straussian GT: a broad question naming the phenomenon, narrowed during coding.
- Constructivist GT: an initial question and sensitising concepts are points of departure; the question is
  revised as the analysis shows what matters.

In every version questions are refined by theoretical sampling: the emerging categories decide what data to
collect next.

## Role of prior theory

Sensitising concepts (Blumer 1954) as points of departure only. Classic GT postpones the literature review;
Charmaz allows it lightly and asks the researcher to state what they bring. Prior theory never provides
categories to apply.

## Reasoning

Inductive at the base, with abductive moments: a surprising finding leads to a new explanation that is checked
again in the data (Charmaz 2014; Timmermans & Tavory 2012 argue for abduction as the core).

## Theory output

A **substantive theory**: a core category and the categories, properties and relations around it, grounded in
confirmed evidence segments; the storyline memo states it. A **formal theory** when the substantive theory is
compared across areas.

## Quality criteria

Fit (categories fit the data), work (they explain), relevance (to the area), modifiability (open to new data);
Charmaz: credibility, originality, resonance, usefulness. Saturation of categories.

## Working with the result

New data are compared with the categories: they add properties, change relations or show a gap, which sends the
study back to theoretical sampling. The theory can be compared with other theories of the area and can give
hypotheses for a quantitative strand.
